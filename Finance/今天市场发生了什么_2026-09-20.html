Fed Resumes Hiking: +25 bp to 3.75%–4.00%, First Increase Since July 2023
Sep 15–16, 2026

The FOMC concluded its September meeting on Wednesday with a 25 bp hike to a target range of 3.75%–4.00% — the first upward move since July 2023 — approved by a 12–0 vote. The statement described economic activity as "expanding at a solid pace" and inflation as "remaining elevated," with the action intended to support "a timelier return" to the 2% inflation target.

The accompanying Summary of Economic Projections turned hawkish: the median end-2026 funds-rate projection rose to 4.1% (from 3.8% in June), with 2026 GDP growth at 2.3%, unemployment at 4.1%, and PCE inflation at 3.7%. Equities slipped around the decision — the S&P 500 pulled back to the 7,585 area and the Nasdaq to roughly 25,982 — before rebounding sharply on Thursday as oil prices fell and bond yields retreated (S&P 500 +1.1% to 7,637.76; Nasdaq 100 +1.73%).

BOJ Raises Rates to 1.25% — Highest in 31 Years — and Flags a Policy Shift
Sep 17–18, 2026

The Bank of Japan ended its two-day meeting on Friday by raising the policy rate by 25 bp to around 1.25% — the highest level since 1995 — after lifting rates to ~1.00% in June. The move, widely expected, was supported by seven of nine board members and marks the shortest interval between hikes since Japan ended negative rates in March 2024.

Governor Kazuo Ueda said the BOJ has "entered a phase where it must pay greater attention to the possibility that underlying inflation could overshoot" the 2% target — "our policy phase has changed." The BOJ also announced it will pause its JGB purchase tapering from April 2027, holding monthly purchases around ¥2 trillion. Markets read the package as a "dovish hike" delivered ahead of a long Japanese holiday weekend; the next meetings are scheduled for October and December.
